CROSS-PLATFORM COMPARISON — SUMMARY
======================================================================

1. RESULTS

Differential expression results from the three datasets were merged on gene symbol,
yielding 13717 genes measured in all three. Genome-wide log2 fold changes (AD − control)
were positively correlated in every pairwise comparison (microarray vs RNA-seq r = 0.41;
microarray vs SEA-AD r = 0.29; RNA-seq vs SEA-AD r = 0.26). Among genes significant in
both datasets of a pair (padj < 0.05, |log2FC| > 0.5), the direction of change agreed for
100/101 (99%) of microarray/RNA-seq genes, 73/73 (100%) of microarray/SEA-AD genes and
56/60 (93%) of RNA-seq/SEA-AD genes. 13 genes were significant in all three datasets. Of
the 33-gene candidate panel, 29 of the 31 genes measured in all three datasets changed in
the same direction in every dataset, with neuronal and neuropeptide genes (e.g. VGF, SST,
NPTX2, SNAP25) decreased and glial genes (e.g. GFAP, GJA1, VIM) increased in AD. Panel
genes reached significance most often in the microarray (18 genes), which had the largest
sample, and least often in SEA-AD (2 genes). Overall, the direction of the AD
transcriptomic signal was reproducible across microarray, bulk RNA-seq and single-nucleus
RNA-seq platforms and across temporal-cortex regions, although effect sizes differed
between datasets.

4. LIMITATIONS

These comparisons have several limitations. The bulk RNA-seq dataset sampled the lateral
temporal lobe whereas the microarray and SEA-AD datasets sampled the middle temporal
gyrus, so regional differences cannot be fully separated from platform differences.
Sample sizes differed tenfold (195 vs 22 vs 20), so absence of significance in the
smaller datasets largely reflects power, and RNA-seq/SEA-AD fold changes were
apeglm-shrunk while microarray fold changes were not, which compresses their magnitudes.
Finally, the SEA-AD whole-tissue pseudobulk is confounded by cell-type composition:
dementia donors contributed fewer nuclei and relatively more glia, so part of the
apparent glial up-regulation and neuronal down-regulation reflects differences in which
cells were sampled rather than changes in expression per cell.
